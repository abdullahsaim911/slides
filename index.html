Problem statement

STEM lectures are built on what learners can see. For blind and low-vision students, much of that content never reaches them.

285million

people worldwide live with a visual impairment

39million

of them are completely blind

70%

of STEM content becomes inaccessible when visuals are not converted

Sources: WHO estimates (Pascolini & Mariotti, 2012); Human-AI Systems for Video Accessibility.

Why current tools fall short

Screen readers stop at text

JAWS and NVDA cannot interpret diagrams, equations or graphs shown in video.

Captions miss the visuals

Speech becomes text, but what is drawn, written and pointed at is lost.

Tools work in isolation

No single system joins speech, visuals, math and gestures into one explanation.

Proposed solution

Synapse brings speech recognition, computer vision, gesture understanding and a large language model together, then speaks a context-aware explanation to the learner.

    Any YouTube STEM lecture

  2. Understand

  4. Explain

What is Synapse?

An accessibility-first lecture companion. It turns the visual half of an online STEM lecture into spoken, grounded explanations, so blind and low-vision learners can follow at the same pace as their classmates.

Lecture understanding

Transcript, frames and instructor cues analysed together

Grounded explanations

Built from visual evidence, not guesses

Adjustable depth

Brief, standard or detailed

Voice and keyboard

What is done

Synapse is built end to end: an accessible interface, a durable backend and the full multimodal pipeline, running in the browser and as a desktop app.

  • Accessible web app with embedded lecture player
  • Durable lecture-processing backend (FastAPI)
  • Transcription from captions or speech recognition
  • Frame analysis, gesture detection and synchronization
  • Vision-language understanding with structured evidence
  • LLM explanation synthesis at three depths
  • Text-to-speech with streamed and saved audio
  • Voice commands and spoken replies
  • Accounts, library, bookmarks and notes
  • Lecture Q&A grounded in the transcript

Challenges

Six problems shaped the design, and how Synapse addresses each.

ChallengeHow Synapse addresses it

Avoiding invented details

Explanations must stay faithful to what the lecture shows and says.

Visual observations become structured evidence; a coverage plan and bounded inputs constrain the explanation.

Choosing what to describe

Describing everything overwhelms; omitting a key relationship confuses.

Coverage planning picks what matters, and learners choose brief, standard or detailed depth.

Managing explanation timing

Extra narration can collide with the instructor's speech.

Synchronization selects intervention moments; playback pauses only when an explanation takes over.

Recovering from partial failures

One failed model step must not make the lecture unusable.

Durable jobs, checkpoints and retry or resume keep finished work and recover the rest.

Balancing time and cost

Deep analysis of long videos needs heavy, repeated model calls.

A three-stage pipeline with caching reuses results and prioritises what the learner asks for.

Building an accessible interface

Navigation, playback, progress and errors must work without sight.

Keyboard shortcuts, voice commands, live-region announcements and an explanation-ready playback gate.
